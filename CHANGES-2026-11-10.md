# Product-readiness pass, November 10, 2026

Starting point: `main` at `684ce8a` (PR #57). Nothing here was deployed, and no production data, Stripe account, or mailbox was touched. Every change is covered by a test; the commands are at the end.

## Fixed: things that would cost money or trust

**1. There was no working way to pay.** The "Renew" buttons on the account screen, and every pay link in the trial-ended, plan-ending, and held-request emails, led to a page that said "sign in, then choose a plan" and linked back to the account screen, which showed the same buttons. Checkout was only reachable by publishing a brand-new page address after the three free jobs were used. A family whose 14-day trial ended had no way to bring the page back.
Now: the email link opens Your account with the plan picked; the account screen and the Go live panel have real Pay / Renew buttons; after Stripe the parent lands back on the account with a confirmation. Paying from the account only confirms the payment; it never republishes the page from whatever draft happens to be on the parent's phone.

**2. Renewing early was impossible.** Reminder emails go out 14 and 3 days before a plan ends, but checkout refused any account that was still paid. A plan can now be renewed in its last 30 days and the new time is added on to the current end date.

**3. A season bought near its end cost full price for a few days.** On August 30 the $9.99 season ran through August 31. When fewer than 30 days are left, the season offer now runs through the end of the next season. The end date is also shown in the product name on the Stripe page.

**4. Picking the other plan charged the first one.** If a parent opened checkout for one plan, went back, and picked the other, the open session for the first plan (and its price) was reused for up to 24 hours. The old session is now closed first.

**5. Payment depended on the webhook alone.** If the Stripe webhook was late or misconfigured, a parent who had paid saw "still processing" indefinitely. The return from Stripe now looks the session up and applies it; the payments ledger keeps the webhook from applying it twice.

**6. The live site serves its own source code and internal notes.** `https://hustlebuilder.org/host/lib/router.mjs`, `/SETUP.md`, `/REPAIR-HANDOFF.md` (which names a customer's child), the test and stress folders, and `package.json` are all publicly readable today, because Vercel serves every file in the repo. `.vercelignore` now keeps tests, stress logs, scripts and the notes out of the deployment, and `vercel.json` redirects requests for `host/` and the package files to the home page. **Check after the next deploy** that those URLs no longer return the files.

**7. Legal text said the opposite of what the product does.** The public privacy page ended with "Have a lawyer read it before real families use hosting" and named internal settings. The in-app About screen said "There are no accounts", "Nothing is sent to a server", "Hustle Builder does not process payments", and "A full Terms of Use and Privacy Policy will be added before this app is sold". The privacy page is rewritten to match the code (including a correction: a neighbor's contact details are stored for about 30 days, not only emailed), there is a new `/terms` page, the About screen is accurate, and the consent step links both. `CONSENT_VERSION` is now `2026-11-10`.

## Fixed: bugs

- **Calendar times were wrong outside Central time.** Every job was written as America/Chicago. The parent's browser time zone is now saved at publish and used for the calendar feed, the `.ics` attachment, and the Google Calendar link. Existing accounts keep Central until they next publish.
- **Link names rejected ordinary business names.** `maya-yard-help`, `sam-dog-walker`, `mia-book-club` were refused ("help", "book", "test", "page" were banned anywhere in the name; any name ending in a word that is also a surname was treated as a last name). Reserved words now apply to the whole name; a short list of impersonation words (admin, support, billing, hustle ...) is still refused anywhere; the surname rule applies only to exactly `first-last`.
- **A title or tagline containing `$$`, `$&` or `` $` `` was mangled** in the link-preview tags.
- **Renewal and trial reminders were skipped if the daily cron missed their exact day.** They now go by window. "in 1 days" is now "tomorrow".
- **Email the provider rejects outright was retried for 30 days.** It is dropped after three tries. Outages and auth problems still retry.
- **The service worker stored a separate 1.4 MB copy of the builder for every link** (`/?draft=…`, `/?ask=…`, `/?account=1`), with those private codes as cache keys, and would cache an error page as the offline copy. It stores one copy and only good responses.
- **Abuse counters were never deleted** (one database row per visitor address per day, forever). They are bucketed by day and swept by the daily cron, along with expired admin sessions.
- **Nothing was ever logged.** Every failure became a silent 503. Failures now log the method, route (with private tokens removed), and error name/code — never a message, address, or payload. Mail failures log the provider status. A missing `RESEND_API_KEY` logs once.

## Second pass, same day: refunds and scale

### Refunds: 14-day money back

The policy you chose is now written into `/terms`, the receipt page, the receipt email, and the account screen: a full refund when a parent asks within 14 days of a payment; after that, none except where the law requires it. `REFUND_DAYS` in `host/lib/router.mjs` is the one number behind all of them.

How a refund works now:

- **From the dashboard.** A paid family's row shows "Refund $19.99" with "day 5 of 14" (or "past 14 days") under it. It opens a confirm page that says whether the payment is inside the window and whether the family has been refunded before. Confirming sends the money back through Stripe and ends the plan that payment bought.
- **From Stripe.** A full refund issued in the Stripe dashboard, or a card dispute, ends the plan the same way. A partial refund changes nothing.
- **What "ends the plan" means.** The account goes back to exactly how it was before that payment: a free trial that still had time resumes, a lapsed plan is lapsed again. If the family renewed afterwards, only the time the refunded payment added is removed. Nothing is deleted. The parent gets one email. The receipt shows "Refunded".
- Refunds are applied once whichever route they come by.

**One setup step for you:** in Stripe, open the webhook endpoint for `/api/stripe/webhook` and add the events `charge.refunded` and `charge.dispute.created`. Until you do, refunds made from this dashboard still work fully; only refunds made inside Stripe would go unnoticed.

Refunds are operator-approved on purpose. A self-serve button would let a family pay, use 13 days, refund, and repeat.

### Scale

Three things used to read every account while holding a lock that paused every other write on the site. None of them does now.

- **The daily job** handles one family at a time in its own short transaction. It works within a time budget and records its place; if it runs out of time, the five-minute mail job continues it until the day's pass is finished. Expired records are cleared in a single database statement.
- **The admin dashboard** builds its list from three bulk reads, shows 50 rows a page with search (link name, email, or business) and a "reported or taken down" filter, and reads booking details only for the rows on screen. It no longer opens each page's HTML (up to 2 MB each): the kid's name and business name are saved on the small page record at publish.
- **Deleting an account** follows what the account points at instead of reading every stored row. Drafts and "ask my parent" links are found through a small index written when they are saved; for 90 days after this release a narrower scan also covers records saved before the index existed, then it switches itself off.
- The database gets a prefix index, created automatically on first use, and the function time limit is set to 60 seconds in `vercel.json`.

Limits that remain: the dashboard still loads every account record (not page content) to compute its summary tiles, which is comfortable into the tens of thousands of families but not beyond. None of this has been run against the hosted database under load; the SQL was exercised on the local engine the test suite uses.

## Not changed, and still yours to decide

1. **A lawyer has not read the new Privacy and Terms pages.** They are plain-language and match what the code does, but they are a draft. That now includes a refund promise you are bound by once it is live. The parental-consent question in SETUP.md ("COPPA, in plain language") is unchanged and still open.
2. **`*.github.io` and `localhost` are still allowed browser origins** for the API. Harmless without a session token, but a leftover from when the builder lived on GitHub Pages. Remove from `allowOrigin` once nothing uses it.
3. **One free trial per email address** can be restarted with a plus-address (`name+2@gmail.com`).
4. **Three reports from three addresses hide a page.** That is quick protection and also a way to grief a kid's page; the dashboard now makes restoring it one click.
5. **`npm audit`** reports a high-severity advisory in `zod`, pulled in by `@vercel/blob` (kept only to read the old Blob store) and by the test browser. It is not on a path that handles visitor input. The clean fix is removing `@vercel/blob` once the frozen Blob backup is no longer needed.
6. Real Stripe purchase/refund, real inbox delivery, and a hosted-Postgres load test are still operator checks, as REPAIR-HANDOFF.md says.

## Before deploying

1. `npm ci && CHROME=/path/to/chrome npm run test:all`
2. Deploy to a preview first. Check: `/privacy`, `/terms`, a kid page, a booking, and that `/SETUP.md` and `/host/lib/router.mjs` no longer load.
3. In Stripe test mode on the preview: let a trial lapse (or set `TRIAL_DAYS=1`), follow the email link, pay, and confirm the page returns. Then switch plans mid-checkout once. Then refund that payment from `/admin` and confirm the money goes back in Stripe and the page returns to its earlier state.
4. Add `charge.refunded` and `charge.dispute.created` to the Stripe webhook endpoint (test and live).
5. Tell existing families nothing changes for them. Sessions and calendar links are not reset by this release.
